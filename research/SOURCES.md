## Usage notes

* **S22** and **S45** are conference proceedings published without an abstract. They are listed only as
  corroborating pointers; no claim in `physiology.json` rests on them alone.
* **S13** is an FAO AGRIS bibliographic record. It was read as a record (title, author, year, summary),
  and is used only for the statement that the dromedary stomach has three compartments and a forestomach
  motility pattern unlike that of true ruminants.
* **S44** (Arabic Encyclopedia) is an Arabic reference work. It is used for: three-compartment stomach
  with no omasum, the "water sacs" misconception, absence of gallbladder, oval red blood cells, hump as a
  fat store, 25 % body-mass loss tolerance, ~100 L drunk in under a quarter of a hour, diurnal body
  temperature 34-41 °C, delayed sweating, concentrated urine and nearly dry faeces.
* **S47** and **S48** are Arabic-language institutional/official pages that describe camels and camelid
  production in general terms. They are used only for contextual framing (why camels matter), never for
  anatomical or physiological numbers.
* Journal articles published in **2026** were retrieved from their publishers' indexes during this
  mission (current date 2026-09-27) and their abstracts/metadata were read directly.

### Round-2 usage notes

* **S54** and **S85** were read as full chapters on the IVIS site (open web pages, not paywalled). They are
  institutional veterinary teaching resources and are used for reproductive-tract anatomy and for the
  soft palate (dulla) respectively.
* **S53** is a conference paper published in an MDPI proceedings journal (`Biol Life Sci Forum`), open
  access, based on a month-by-month follow-up of 70 camels over their lives. It is the only source
  reached in this round that states the **full** dromedary dental formula (22 deciduous, 34 permanent,
  with the jaw-by-jaw counts). It is treated as one moderate-authority source, and the audit says so;
  **S52** is used alongside it for dental disorders and age estimation but does not itself print the
  formula.
* **S65**, **S66** and **S68** are studies on the **Bactrian** camel, not the dromedary. They are used
  only where the mechanism is described (poll-gland metabolism in the rut; adrenal zonation) and the
  affected claims are marked as camelid-level, not dromedary-specific, in `AUDIT.md` and `NOTES.md`.
  The dromedary-specific poll-gland study is **S64**.
* **S76** is a study on the **guanaco**, a South American camelid. It is used only for the general
  camelid structure of the digital cushion; the dromedary-specific foot material is **S78**.
* **S81** and **S83** are papers in the *Journal of Camel Practice and Research* whose abstracts were
  read from publisher metadata (full text is paywalled). They are used only for what the abstract
  states (seven cervical vertebrae and cervical proportions; CT appearance of the eye) and are flagged
  as metadata-level in the notes.
* **S82** is cited from the two published abstracts of Parts I and II (immature and adult skulls).
* **S75** is the same Dagg (1974) locomotion paper the project already uses as its `dagg` source; it was
  added here under an S-number so that the research file can cite it directly.
* **S72** (camel tonsils) and **S85** (soft palate) are used together for the throat/soft-palate entries;
  **S73** supplies the one clinical report of soft-palate prolapse and is not used for any number.
* **S86** was added late in the round, during the audit, because the round-1 dataset had no
  dromedary-specific hydatidosis figures. It is the source of the two corrections in `AUDIT.md`
  (lung 87.87 %, liver 9 % of hydatid infections in 152 camels).
* **S87** was added during the audit for the nasal bot fly of camels (*Cephalopina titillator*,
  nasopharyngeal myiasis), which verifies the atlas's "nasal bot larvae attach at the pharynx" line.
